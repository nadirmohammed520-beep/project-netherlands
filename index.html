Inside Dutch Prisons

Rehabilitation & Humane Treatment

The Netherlands structures its correctional facilities around human dignity, self-sufficiency, and active social recovery.

1. Living Spaces & Cells

Cells measure approximately 5m long x 2m wide x 2.5m high.

  • Private restroom, desk, bed, chair, and TV.
  • Inmates cook their own meals under supervision.
  • Allowed to wear their own personal clothing.

2. Daily Routine & Culture

Daily life is designed to mimic normal outside routines as closely as possible to build healthy habits.

  • Daily sports, library time, and outdoor breaks.
  • Weekly family visits and regular phone calls.
  • Facility jobs build workplace discipline.

3. Programs & Mental Health

Addressing root behavioral issues and mental health prevents future crime.

  • Specialized psychiatric treatment programs (TBS).
  • Life-skills, counseling, and youth support.
  • Post-release staff guidance for smooth reentry.

My Policy Proposal for U.S. Reform

Make U.S. Prisons More Livable & Rehabilitation-Focused

The U.S. should redesign its facilities and sentencing laws to prioritize resocialization and humane living conditions rather than long-term isolation.

Education & Skills

Invest in job training, formal schooling, and mental health therapy inside all prison facilities.

Alternatives

Use community service and electronic monitoring for non-violent offenses instead of prison time.

Limit Confinement

Restrict solitary confinement and harsh sensory deprivation to protect inmate mental health.

Why Would This Change Help?

It directly reduces recidivism rates. When prisoners leave with job skills, counseling, and social support, they transition smoothly into normal life rather than returning to crime. This creates safer communities and saves millions in taxpayer money.

The Netherlands has an incarceration rate of 70 to 100 per 100,000 citizens. The total prison population is 12,570 inmates out of an official capacity of 14,789 (78.0% occupancy rate). Demographics include:
  • 29.2% Pre-trial detainees
  • 25.2% Foreign prisoners
  • 4.7% Female prisoners
  • 1.3% Juveniles under 18 years old
Around 35% of former prisoners reoffend within 1 year, and 47% to 49% reoffend within 2 years. However, across all court-ordered sanctions, the 2-year reconviction rate drops significantly to 27% - 30%.
The core philosophy is rehabilitation and reintegration. The system works toward building a safer society by combining fair punishment with psychiatric treatment, health care, education, and social recovery starting from day one of imprisonment.
Standard cells measure 5 meters long, 2 meters wide, and 2.5 meters high. Each cell includes a bed, desk, chair, wardrobe, small television, and private restroom. Inmates are even permitted to cook their own meals under staff supervision.
Daily life is kept as close to normal life as possible. Inmates participate in sports, library time, daily outdoor breaks, weekly visits from family, and phone calls. Many work facility jobs to build positive routines. Because facilities aren't overcrowded, living conditions remain clean and calm.
Programs focus on behavior correction. Inmates with mental health issues enter special forensic psychiatric programs (TBS). Counseling, life skills, and youth education are widely available, alongside dedicated staff who guide prisoners during their reentry into society.
The Netherlands operates under a Civil Law system based on written statutes, with trials decided by professional judges (often 3 judges) and no juries. The U.S. uses a Common Law system where judicial precedents carry immense weight, and 12-citizen juries decide guilt or innocence.
The Netherlands has significantly lower recidivism (25%-30%) compared to the U.S. (40%-50% returning within 3-5 years). Dutch incarceration is around 70 per 100k versus the U.S. at 600-700 per 100k. Declining Dutch crime rates even allowed them to rent empty prison cells to neighboring countries!
The U.S. should redesign its prisons to be more livable and focused on resocialization. This includes expanding job/mental health programs, increasing non-prison alternatives (community service), and severely restricting solitary confinement.
It lowers repeat crime rates, prepares individuals for gainful employment, ensures safer neighborhoods, and reduces the massive financial tax burden of operating overpopulated prisons.
